Medical Reference Guide

The Facts About Skin Cancer

Appearance varies widely across skin types and UV exposures. Any new, bleeding, or unusual spot should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

Melanoma

Melanoma

Melanoma is an aggressive and potentially life-threatening type of skin cancer that develops in melanocyte pigment cells. Early identification is crucial before cells penetrate deep vascular tissue.

Often asymmetric with irregular borders
Can develop on sun-exposed or unexposed skin
Basal Cell Carcinoma

Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)

The most common form of skin cancer. BCCs typically emerge on areas regularly exposed to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, presenting as pearly papules, non-healing sores, or pinkish patches.

May bleed easily or ooze and crust over
Eligible for non-invasive treatments if caught early
Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)

Squamous cell skin cancers often appear as firm red nodules, scaly crusted sores, or open wounds that never fully resolve. They grow slowly but can metastasize if left unchecked.

Crater-like, rough, or elevated wart-like surface
High cure rates when addressed in initial stages
